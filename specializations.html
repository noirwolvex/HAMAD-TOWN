BAHRAIN SECONDARY SYSTEM

A closer look at the system and your next steps.

Explore the framework, compare each pathway and review the university and scholarship information in one place.

This expanded guide is based on the supplied study. Requirements, percentages, fees and scholarship conditions can change; confirm them with your school, the Ministry or the relevant institution before making a decision.

01Secondary framework and common core
THE SCHOOL STAGE

Grades 10 to 12

The secondary stage in Bahrain covers Grades 10–12 and is generally intended for students aged 15–18.

The supplied study describes a credit-based unified pathways system, designed to give students a shared academic and cultural foundation before they focus on the Scientific, Commercial or Literary track.

COMMON FOUNDATION

The Grade 10 common core

All students begin with shared core subjects, including languages, sciences, mathematics, humanities and social studies, and Islamic education.

The study says students select a track through the Ministry of Education's student information platform, eduSIS, after completing Grade 9 and the first secondary level.

Community service in the supplied study

Community service is listed as a graduation requirement for regular students in Grade 11: 4 credit hours, equivalent to 60 hours. The hours are divided into 30 hours within the school and 30 hours with external public- or private-sector organisations. The material says affiliation and home-study students are exempt.

SEMESTER GPA

Weighted course average

Multiply each course grade by its credit hours, add the results, then divide by the total registered credit hours.

Semester GPA = Σ (Course grade × Credit hours) ÷ Σ (Credit hours)
CUMULATIVE GPA

Overall secondary average

The same weighted method is applied to courses completed across secondary school. The supplied study identifies the cumulative average as an important factor in university and scholarship competition.

Cumulative GPA = Σ (All course grades × Credit hours) ÷ Σ (All credit hours)

Scientific track

The study identifies two subject combinations: Physics and Mathematics, or Chemistry and Biology. It describes this track as the broadest in university flexibility, while also carrying the highest academic workload.

Commercial track

It focuses on finance, accounting, business, entrepreneurship and logistics-related directions. The study describes its workload as moderate and notes its potential for steady cumulative achievement.

Literary track

It suits students interested in reading, language, history and society, with possible directions including law, media, languages, history, education and social services.

04Universities and programme entry requirements
NATIONAL UNIVERSITY

University of Bahrain

Competitive admission

The supplied study describes admission as competitive and dependent on available places, cumulative average and track compatibility.

70%General minimum secondary average stated in the study.
66.66%Alternative minimum stated for applicants with disabilities.
2 yearsMaximum time since graduation mentioned in the supplied material.

The material lists a credit-hour fee of BHD 8 for Bahraini students and BHD 80 for non-Bahraini students. Fees and exemptions should be checked directly with the university.

COMPETITIVE CALCULATION

Best eight grades

The study says the competitive percentage is the average of the student's eight highest secondary grades, provided the set includes the core subjects required by the college.

Competitive percentage = (Sum of the 8 selected grades) ÷ 8

For Engineering, Science and Information Technology, Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry must be among the selected subjects, according to the material.

PRIVATE-SCHOOL APPLICANTS

Equivalent track classification

Private-school applicants are classified by the subject group with the most completed courses. If the number of courses is tied, the student's selected track is used, according to the supplied study.

Bahrain Polytechnic

The study describes a practical model built around technical skills and field projects.

  • It lists a minimum range of 60%–70%, depending on the programme and competition.
  • Selection may include English and Mathematics entrance tests.
  • Some programmes may also require a personal interview.

MEDICAL AND HEALTH SCIENCES

Arabian Gulf University

For Medicine and Surgery, the study lists the Scientific track and a cumulative average of at least 95%.

  • It lists 90% or higher in Biology, Chemistry, Physics and Advanced Mathematics, with 85% described as an absolute lower threshold.
  • It mentions IELTS 6.0 or 6.5, a medical aptitude test, an interview and a medical examination.
  • Other medical programmes are described as having minimum averages in the 85%–90% range.
05Government scholarships, grants and track changes
FULL SCHOLARSHIPS90% and above

The study says full scholarships cover tuition and provide a financial allowance, and are awarded through competition among students with averages of 90% or higher. It also states that students with 95% or higher are guaranteed their first-choice scholarship when studying at the University of Bahrain or an institution included in the annual plan.

FINANCIAL GRANTS90% and above

Students who meet the 90% threshold but do not receive a full scholarship may be considered for an annual financial grant, paid in two instalments. Renewal requires a transcript and proof of registration before each semester.

CHILDREN OF MINISTRY EMPLOYEES75% and above

The supplied study lists a minimum average of 75%, at least 10 years of continuous service by the parent at the Ministry of Education, and approved admission to an eligible University of Bahrain programme.

CHANGING YOUR SECONDARY TRACK

What happens when a student transfers?

  1. 01

    Specialised grades from the previous track are removed, while grades for shared subjects are retained, according to the supplied study.

  2. 02

    The student completes the new track's specialised courses through self-study using the affiliation or home-study route, then sits the required examinations.

  3. 03

    The new courses must be passed for their credit hours to count. Depending on readiness and results, the transfer may extend secondary study or affect the cumulative average.

06Strategic takeaways and early planning

Scientific: widest range, heavier workload

The study presents the Scientific track as the broadest route to university programmes, including Medicine, Engineering, IT, Business and Humanities. It also highlights the demanding mathematical and practical workload and the importance of protecting the cumulative average.

Commercial: business and financial directions

It points towards finance, accounting, logistics and selected IT programmes. The study describes it as a route that may support steady academic performance, while not providing direct access to Medicine and Engineering.

Literary: law, media and humanities

It is presented as a route towards Law, Media, Languages, History, Education and social or humanities-related fields, with a strong emphasis on written expression and textual analysis.

Plan before selecting your track.

The study recommends starting in Grade 9: compare your interests and strengths with university requirements and future areas of work. It highlights financial technology, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, health sciences and advanced engineering as areas connected with Bahrain's development priorities.

08Academic assessment and the demands of each pathway

The following comparison describes the kinds of learning, preparation and assessment demands associated with each pathway. It is a qualitative educational analysis, not an official Ministry ranking of difficulty.

SCIENTIFIC PATHWAY

High cumulative conceptual demand

Physics, Mathematics and the other sciences build on earlier concepts. A secure foundation in Grade 10 can make later topics easier to understand, while gaps in prerequisite knowledge may become more noticeable in Grades 11 and 12.

Examinations may require students to combine formulas, solve unfamiliar problems and manage time carefully during multi-step questions.

COMMERCIAL PATHWAY

Structured and procedure-focused learning

Accounting and finance often rely on clear conventions, organised steps and repeated application. Examples include journal entries, financial records, budgets and interpreting business information.

Regular practice, accuracy and showing each step of a solution are important. Familiarity with procedures can help students prepare systematically, but it does not guarantee a particular grade or cumulative average.

LITERARY PATHWAY

Reading, interpretation and written analysis

This pathway places substantial emphasis on reading comprehension, remembering key concepts and analysing language, historical events and ideas across longer texts.

Assessments commonly include essays, textual interpretation and structured explanations. Clear expression, relevant evidence and a well-organised argument matter; achieving full marks depends on the requirements of each question and marking criteria.

No pathway is automatically easy or difficult for every student.

Personal interests, prior foundations, study habits, subject strengths and the specific programme all influence the experience. Choose a pathway by considering both your abilities and the entry requirements of the university programmes you may want to pursue.

IMPORTANT ADMISSION NOTE

Your track is a starting point, not a university admission guarantee.

University admission requirements vary by programme. They may include a required school track, specific subjects, a minimum average, an admission test or an interview. Always check the latest requirements before choosing your subjects.
